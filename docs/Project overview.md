# Project Overview

## 1. Project Background

BINNSIE is an educational smart recycling project developed in response to the challenges of waste management and recycling in Panama.

The project recognizes that recycling is not only a matter of having containers available. It also involves how people interact with waste, understand material separation, and participate in responsible waste management.

BINNSIE approaches this challenge through the combination of technology, education, and environmental awareness, with an initial focus on school environments.

## 2. The Core Idea

**The bin sorts, the student learns.**

This principle represents the central idea behind BINNSIE.

Instead of placing the entire responsibility for sorting materials on students, BINNSIE aims to make the separation process more accessible through an automated system.

At the same time, the process becomes an opportunity for students to observe, understand, and engage with recycling.

The objective is not simply to collect recyclable materials, but to make recycling a more understandable and participatory experience.

## 3. Design Philosophy

BINNSIE is guided by four main principles:

- **Learning through interaction:** Recycling should be an experience that helps students understand how materials are separated and why it matters.
- **Transparency:** Making the sorting process visible can help students understand what happens after an item is deposited.
- **Technology with purpose:** Technology should address practical environmental challenges while supporting education.
- **Participation:** Students should have opportunities to engage with recycling beyond simply disposing of materials.

## 4. A System-Based Approach

BINNSIE considers recycling as a process that involves more than the initial disposal of an item.

Effective recycling requires attention to different stages: material identification, separation, user participation, collection, and appropriate handling after collection.

For this reason, BINNSIE brings together several complementary areas:

- Physical engineering.
- Artificial intelligence.
- Digital interaction.
- Environmental education.
- Recycling management.

These areas are intended to work together as parts of a broader system rather than as isolated solutions.

## 5. Target Environment

BINNSIE is initially focused on schools, where recycling can become part of everyday learning and environmental awareness.

Schools provide an opportunity to introduce recycling practices in a shared environment, encourage student participation, and build habits through repeated interaction.

Although the initial focus is educational institutions, the project's broader approach may be adapted to other environments in the future.

## 6. Long-Term Vision

BINNSIE aims to develop an integrated recycling experience in which automated material separation, immediate feedback, educational engagement, and responsible material management complement one another.

The long-term vision is to help make recycling more accessible, understandable, and engaging while encouraging a stronger culture of environmental responsibility.

This vision will continue to evolve through prototyping, testing, feedback, and collaboration.

## 7. Project Principles

BINNSIE's development is guided by the following principles:

- Environmental responsibility.
- Education and awareness.
- Practical and purposeful innovation.
- Student participation.
- Transparency and accountability.
- Continuous improvement.

**BINNSIE — The bin sorts, the student learns.**
